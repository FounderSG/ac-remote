AC Remote is an air conditioner remote control for iPhone and iPad. It sends infrared signals through an IR transmitter plugged into the headphone output. This page covers what you need, how to add an air conditioner, and what to try when it does not answer.

What you need

An IR transmitter that plugs into the headphone output: a small infrared emitter on a 3.5 mm plug, driven by the sound the app plays. On an iPhone or iPad without a headphone jack, plug it into a Lightning or USB-C to 3.5 mm headphone adapter. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi IR blasters do not work with the app.

Adding an air conditioner

  1. Plug in the IR transmitter.
  2. In the list of air conditioners, tap + and choose the brand. You can search for it by name.
  3. Point the transmitter at the air conditioner from close by, and press the power key of each remote in the list, one after another, until the air conditioner answers.
  4. Tap Choose on the remote it answered. Choose appears once you have pressed that remote's power key. Tap a remote to see all its models.

Some remotes have a single power key that turns the air conditioner on and off in turn. With these, each press switches it, as the remote's key does, and the app cannot know whether it is on or off.

Using the panel

  • Each change you make on the panel is sent to the air conditioner. The bar at the bottom shows when the last one was sent. Resend sends the settings again, for example when the air conditioner did not hear them.
  • Some remotes send nothing while the air conditioner is off. The settings you change then go out when you turn it on, as the bar says.
  • The panel shows what the app sent last. It does not see changes made with the air conditioner's own remote control.
  • The More menu at the top right renames the air conditioner, sets its temperature unit, resets its settings and deletes it.

Holding an iPhone upside down

The transmitter plugs into the bottom of the iPhone, so the screen is upside down when you point it at the air conditioner. Tap the up-and-down arrows at the top right (Turn upside down) to turn the app's screen around, and tap them again to turn it upright. The app remembers your choice. An iPad turns its screen around by itself, so it has no such button.

When the air conditioner does not answer

  • Check that the transmitter is plugged in all the way. If the app says no IR transmitter is plugged in although it is, the sound is going somewhere else, such as Bluetooth headphones, a car or an AirPlay speaker: choose the headphones as the output in Control Center, or disconnect the other device.
  • The app turns the volume up to full before each send. If it says a command was sent below full volume, a volume limit is holding it back: turn the limit off under Settings › Sounds & Haptics › Headphone Safety.
  • Point the transmitter at the air conditioner's receiver, usually behind a small dark window on the indoor unit, from close by. Infrared light does not pass through walls.
  • If it answers some commands but not others, another remote of the same brand may fit it better: add it again and try more of the remotes in the list.
  • At the end of a brand's list, Not supported yet names models whose codes the app cannot send yet. If the air conditioner answers none of the remotes and its model is there, the app cannot control it yet.
